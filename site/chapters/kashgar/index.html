西域喀什

本段地点

正在展开西域地理…

一 · 汤因比的来世

一个中国古代文人不管漂泊何处，晚年最大的向往就是回归故乡。这事到了近代那些具有世界历史视野的学者那里就不一样了，他们会以一生的学养把时间和空间浓缩，然后拄着拐杖站在书房的窗口看着远方。他们在想：如果生命能够重来一次，我最希望投生何处？

我很想知道几位大学者对这个问题的答案，排在第一的是英国历史学家汤因比（Arnold Joseph Toynbee）。因为正是他洋洋洒洒的着作，最早让我了解了世界各地的不同历史形态。

但是，他已经去世三十多年，似乎并没有留下这方面的答案。我，只能在他的着作中猜测。猜测了几处，都没有把握。

终于，我突然知道，他曾经在一次对话中，留下了答案。

他说，如果生命能够重来一次，他希望生活在中国古代的西域。因为，那是一个文化会聚的福地。

他所说的西域，是指中国新疆塔里木河、叶尔羌河一带。

二 · 文明交汇

我每次去新疆，总会想起汤因比的选择。

西域，这是一个伟大的地名。汉武帝派张骞“通西域”，是这位帝王，也是整个汉代对世界历史的杰出贡献。从此，人类各大文明在那里发生了最大规模的汇集、交流和融合。

本来，无论是印度文明、波斯文明、巴比伦文明、阿拉伯文明，还是再远一点的埃及文明、希腊文明、罗马文明等等，都自成规模、自享尊荣，很难放得下架子来与其他文明主动融合，除非用战争的方式来收纳别人。因此，各大文明都在万分警惕地防范着来自别处的铁骑战火。但是，商品流通的诱惑太大了，旅行者口中的描述太吸引人了，因此，彼此都悄悄地产生了一种不约而同的渴望：要找一个地方，展开各大文明之间的非战争交往。

这个地方需要具备两个条件：一，必须是一个地广人稀的所在，离各大文明的首府都比较遥远，使谁也感受不到威胁；二，所有的旅行团队最想靠近的那个文明，有一种让大家放心的宽容精神。

能够满足这两个条件的地方，在古代世界的地面上只有一个，那就是西域。于是，在天山、昆仑山和塔里木盆地之间的茫茫大漠，终于成了各大文明沟通的巨大平台。看似最缺少文化的地方，变成了最热闹的文化集市。一场场古代的世博会、交易会、嘉年华，不断地在西域开幕又闭幕，闭幕又开幕。

这么一想，觉得汤因比对那里的选择，实在很有道理。

古代由西域通向整个亚洲腹地，有北疆的草原之路和南疆的丝绸之路。丝绸之路又分南、北两路，然后在一个地方汇合，翻越帕米尔高原而继续前去。两条丝绸之路的汇合处，是西域开发最早的城郭叫“疏勒”，也就是现在中国最西的城市喀什，又叫喀什噶尔。

这是历来所有的旅行家、探险家、行脚僧、商贸者都必须停步的地方。不管是出去还是进来，都已经承受过严酷的生死考验，而前面，可能是帕米尔，也可能是塔克拉玛干，考验更大。因此，要在这里收拾一下好不容易捡回来的一条命，然后重新豁命前行。

对很多人来说，这里是生命的最后一站；对另一些人来说，这又是豪迈壮行的新起点。不管是终点还是起点，都是英雄们泼酒祭奠之处。喀什的每一寸空气，都熔铸过男子汉低哑的喉音。

世界在这里渴望着被一次次走通，而高原在这里却显得寸步难行。一位高大的当地汉子在昆仑山脚下对我说：“在这里，地远路险，从有些村子到乡里去，骑毛驴也要走七天。一个妻子最高的愿望是去一趟县城，丈夫不让，说这么漂亮的女人走那么久，怎么还回得来？几十年后丈夫去世，妻子也走不动了。”

但是，这些妻子和丈夫都看到了，总有一些人从他们村边走过。是去乡里吗？是去县城吗？难道，还有更远的地方？

最近，我和妻子又一次去了喀什。一路上饱满的感觉无与伦比，我只想重复多年前说过的一句话：如果你想研究的历史不是一般的历史而是“大历史”，如果你想从事的文学不是一般的文学而是“大文学”，那么，请务必多去西域，多去新疆，多去喀什。

三 · 中心的中心

两千多年前张骞通西域的时候，已经发现喀什有非常像样的商贸市场。后来，出任汉朝“西域都护”的班超，又曾把这里当做安定西域的大本营，他自己一住就是十几年。

班超在这里的时候，当地民众在精神文化上还停留于萨满巫术的原始自然宗教。但是，就在班超走后不久，一件重大的文化事件把这里裹卷进去了：印度的佛教开始向中国大规模传播，这里成了一条最主要的走廊。

对于佛教东传这件事，我一直认为是人类文化史上的一个特大事件。原因是，作为被传入一方的中国大地，自从诸子百家之后已经实现了超浓度的精神自足，似乎一切思维缝隙都已填满，怎么可能如此虔诚地接受万里关山之外一种全然陌生的文明呢？但是，由于印度文明和中华文明的双向高贵，又痛又痒的防范心理居然被一步步克服。首感痛痒的地方，应该就在喀什。首度克服的地方，应该也在喀什。

磨合了两百年，到了公元四世纪，这儿已经成了一个佛教繁盛之地，留下的古迹和事迹都很多。例如，那位在中国佛教史上贡献堪比玄奘的鸠摩罗什，就曾在十二岁时到这里学习小乘佛教长达两年，后来也在这里，遇到了精通大乘佛教的来自莎车的王子参军兄弟二人，开始转向大乘佛教，并终生传习。而莎车，现在也属喀什地区。尽管喀什的佛教主流一直是小乘，鸠摩罗什不得不离开，但这儿是他的精神转型地。

在鸠摩罗什之后不久，法显西行取经也经过这里，惊叹这里的法会隆重。后来玄奘取经回来时经卷落水破损，也曾在这里停留一段时间补抄。

在公元九世纪至十三世纪的喀喇汗王朝时期，喀什表现了很高的文化创造能力，向世界贡献了第一部用纯粹回鹘文写成的长篇叙事诗《福乐智慧》和精心巨着《突厥语大词典》。这是两部极重要的维吾尔文化经典，跟着它们，还有不少优秀的着作产生。喀什，因创建经典而闪现出神圣的光彩。

其实，伊斯兰教在公元七世纪传入中国时，也以喀什为前沿。在这里落地生根几百年后，才向北疆传播。喀什地区的伊斯兰教文物不胜枚举，因为直到今天这儿的主要信仰还是这个宗教。千余年来天天被虔诚的仪式滋润着，即便是遗迹也成了生活，因此看上去都神采奕奕。

据到过这里的欧洲旅行家马可·波罗记述，基督教的一个教派聂斯托利派即中国所称“景教”，在这里也不乏信奉者，而且礼拜完满，尽管这个教派早在公元五世纪已在罗马被取缔。对此，作为意大利人的马可·波罗就很敏感。同样，在古代波斯早被取缔的祆教（即拜火教），在这一带的民间也曾风行，致使《南唐书》说疏勒地区“俗奉祆神”。

总之，几千年来，喀什不仅是商品贸易的集散地，而且也是精神文化的集散地。集散范围很大，近至中亚、南亚，远至西亚、欧洲。如果说，西域是几大文明的交汇中心，那么，喀什则是中心的中心。

这个地位，自古以来一直具有，却只是默默地存在于各国商人心中。到了十九世纪，世界在空间和时间上获得新的自觉，喀什的重要性再一次被广泛瞩目。当时很多全球顶级的学者都坚信，这一带必定留下了诸多文明的重大脚印，因此都不远万里纷纷赶来。正如日本探险家橘瑞超所说的那样：“这是中亚地区政治、商业的中心，自古以来就为世人所知，至今到中亚旅行的人，没有不介绍喀什的。”

翻阅那时的世界考古学着作就可以发现，喀什，在东方学研究中，已经成了一个怎么也避不开的常用名词。

到十九世纪末、二十世纪初，中国内忧外患，水深火热，差一点被列强彻底瓜分了。但是，即使到了这个时候，一个以亚洲腹地为目标的考古学家如果没有来过喀什，还是会像一个毕业生的文凭上没有盖过校长的签名印章。

历史，很容易被遗忘，却又很难被彻底遗忘。在那些迷乱的夜晚，正当一批批外来的酒徒在沙丘上狂欢喧嚣的时候，他们脚下，沙丘寂寞一叹，冷然露出某个历史大器的残角，似乎在提醒他们，这是什么地方。

四 · 两座领事馆

一八八一年四月，俄国驻喀什领事馆开张，本来这很正常，但奇怪的是，领事馆里有六十名哥萨克骑兵。这些骑兵每天早晚两次列队穿越市区的大广场到城东河边操练，还向围观的人群表演刀术、马术、射击术。俄国驻喀什的领事很有学问，名叫彼得罗夫斯基，一个英国学者曾这样描述他：

彼得罗夫斯基是个能干、傲慢、狡猾而精于诱惑的家伙，任职的二十一年间对中国官员使尽了阴谋恐吓、威胁、利诱、收买、强迫之伎俩。他的目的便是将新疆最西部的绿洲从中国瓜分出去，使俄国得以控制通往印度后门的战略性山口。

（珍妮特·米斯基：《斯坦因考古与探险》）

俄国要控制通往印度的后门，显然是在挑衅英国。当时，英国不仅在印度实行殖民统治，而且已经控制了昆仑山、兴都库什山、阿姆河以南的多数地区，怎么会允许俄国来插手？因此，后起的英国驻喀什总领事馆占地面积，是俄国领事馆的整整两倍，而且也比英国自己在乌鲁木齐的领事馆豪华很多。一位英国记者写道：

在大英帝国与沙皇俄国争夺中亚的五十多年大角逐中，喀什一直是大英帝国最前沿的一个阵地。在那场大角逐中，大英帝国为了在亚洲取得政治和经济的主导权，与沙皇俄国进行过漫长而又扑朔迷离的争斗。在大英帝国驻喀什领事馆上飘扬的那面英国国旗，是印度到北极之间唯一的一面。

（彼得·霍布科克：《一个外交官夫人对喀什的回忆》）

就在那队哥萨克骑兵和那面英国国旗天天都在喀什对峙的时候，一些心在千年之前的学者也来到了这座城市。斯文·赫定来了，并从这里出发，发现了千年前的古城丹丹乌里克，又考察了塔里木河和罗布泊的迁徙遗址。斯坦因也来了，顺着斯文·赫定的成果进一步发现了“希腊化的佛教艺术”犍陀罗的遗存，又发现了楼兰遗址……这一系列文物，从不同方向展示了这片土地在古代无与伦比的重要性。

“在古代无与伦比的重要性”，可分为两类。第一类是随着古代的结束而结束，第二类却可以延伸到现代。西域发现的文物，大多属于第二类。它们像古代智者留下的一排排巨大的数学公式，证明着几个大空间之间的必然联系，以及把这种必然联系打通的实际可能。因此，就在这些西域考古大发现之后，历史学家威尔斯作出判断：“直到今天我才开始明白，塔里木河流域比约旦河流域和莱茵河流域更为重要。”

正是这种判断，使得喀什城里那队哥萨克骑兵和那面英国国旗更加抖擞起来。两国的领事，都会殷勤地接待那些考古学家，希望他们为帝国的现代野心提供更多的古代理由。但是，从种种记录来看，那些考古学家对于两位领事除了感谢之外并不抱有太多的尊敬。他们毕竟深谙历史，比眼前披着外交套装的情报政客更知道轻重。他们来到沙漠深处，只要见到一点点古代的痕迹就会急速地跪下双腿，用双手轻轻地扒挖，细细地拂拭。很久很久，还跪在那里。

如果仅仅从动作上看，考古学家，是在代表现代人跪身谢恩。

无言的大地，有多少地方值得我们跪身，又有多少地方需要我们谢恩。

五 · 白色旗幡

在中华文明的诸多“老祖宗”中，在形态和气度上最让人震撼的，是西域，包括喀什。

这个说法也许会使别的“老祖宗”侧目，那实在对不起了，但我实在不是随口赞誉。请想一想，天山、昆仑山和塔克拉玛干大沙漠，这几宗真正的天下巨构，只须窥得其中任何一角，就足以让世人凝神屏息。但在这里，却齐齐地排列在一起、交接在一起、呼应在一起，这会是什么景象？

一连串无可超越的绝境，一重重无与伦比的壮美，一系列无法复制的伟大，包围着你，征服着你，粉碎着你，又收纳着你。你失去了，好不容易重新找回，却是另一个你。

在天山、昆仑山面前，其他“老祖宗”所背靠的三山五岳，就有点像盆景了。在塔克拉玛干大沙漠面前，其他“老祖宗”所吟咏的大漠孤烟、长河落日，也有点太孩子气了。

到喀什，不能按照内地休闲的习惯，选择那些人群密集的旅游景点。应该选择的，是乔戈里峰、慕士塔格冰川和奥依塔克冰川、红其拉甫口岸、亚克艾日克烽火台，以及散布处处的千年胡杨林和夕阳下的沙漠。我和妻子则非常着迷莎车的《十二木卡姆》，每次都听得情醉神驰。难怪躲得那么僻远的它，早已被堂皇地列入世界非物质文化遗产名录。它让我联想到，在隋唐年间轰动长安的疏勒乐和龟兹乐。不错，在中国古代最伟大王朝的雄伟和声中，占据极高引领地位的，大多是西域乐舞。

由此想到，在喀什之外，新疆还有不少西域名胜值得一再拜访，例如龟兹（现在的库车）、于阗（现在的和田）、高昌、交河等地。有足够体力的，还可以狠狠心去一下楼兰、米兰、尼雅遗址。

在叶尔羌河畔，一位本地官员已经摆好了毛笔和宣纸，要我题写几个字，准备刻在山壁上。我问他写哪几个字，他说——

昆仑第一城

我说：“你们这儿，随口一说就气势非凡。”

写完，我的目光越过灿如火阵的胡杨林，再越过层层叠叠的绕山云，远眺昆仑山上的天路。那条天路，通向西藏阿里地区。突然发现，在连绵的雪峰之上，竟然冒出缕缕白烟，飘向蓝天。难道，那里还有人间的生活？

“那么高的云层之上，怎么会有白烟？”我问。

主人说，那不是白烟，而是高天风流吹起了山顶积雪。

原来如此。但转念一想，我刚刚的疑惑，历代旅行者也一定产生过。他们猜测着，判断着，时不时低头看路，又时不时抬起头来。没有人烟的地方何来人烟？他们多半找不到人询问，带着疑惑离开，然后又回头，看了又看。

那么，这神奇的“白烟”，也就成了一面面逗引远方客人的白色旗幡。

想到这里我笑了，心想汤因比先生向往西域的来世之魂，现在一定已经顺着这白色旗幡找到归宿，乐滋滋地安顿了下来。

I · Toynbee’s next life

NO MATTER HOW FAR an ancient Chinese writer traveled, his greatest dream was to return home. However, it is completely different for scholars with the world-historical views of modern times. They would concentrate time and space in their lifetime of cultivation, then walk with a cane to the window of their study and stare into the distance. They would think: where would be my favorite birthplace if I could have my life again?

I have a strong desire to know the answers of some great scholars to this question. The first one is Arnold Joseph Toynbee, an historian from England. Just from his voluminous works, I came to understand the different historic morphologies in the world at the earliest time.

However, he has been dead for more than thirty years, and seems not to leave any answer about it. I can only guess from his works. There are several places, but I am not very sure.

Finally, I know all at once. He disclosed the answer in a conversation.

He said that if he could have his life again, he hoped to live in the West Regions in China, for that was a blessed place for cultural integration.

The West Regions, he said, refers to the area of Tarim River and Yarkand River in Xinjiang, China.

II · Where civilizations met

EVERY TIME I GO TO XINJIANG, I always think of Toynbee’s choice.

The West Regions is really a great name. Emperor Wudi assigned Zhang Qian to “connect the West Regions.” It was this emperor and the overall Han Dynasty that made great contributions to world history. Hence, major civilizations at the greatest scale of assembly, exchange, and integration were here.

Originally, Indian, Persian, Babylon, and Arabic civilizations, or slightly farther back, Egyptian, Greek, and Roman civilizations, could form their own values and enjoy their own glories, but it was very difficult for them to integrate with other civilizations actively, unless taking others through war. Therefore, various major civilizations were cautious and intent on warding off the cavalries and wars from other places. However, commodity circulation was a great lure, and descriptions by travelers were very attractive, so a desire was generated invariably and secretly among them: to find a place and develop peaceful contacts between major civilizations.

Two conditions were necessary. First, it had to be a vast territory with a sparse population, remote from the capital of each civilization, where no one could feel threatened. Second, the civilization that all of the traveling representatives wanted to get close to had to possess the spirit of tolerance that makes people at ease.

There was only one place that could satisfy those two conditions in the ancient world, and that is the West Regions. Thus the vast desert between Mount Tianshan, Mount Kunlun and Tarim Basin finally became a huge platform for the major civilizations of the world to communicate on. The place with a seeming lack of culture became the liveliest culture bazaar. One after another exposition, trade fair, and carnival in ancient times opened and flourished and closed in the West Regions continuously.

Thinking about it for a while, I decided that Toynbee’s selection was very reasonable.

In ancient times, the West Regions could lead to the whole interior of Asia, including the grassland road in northern Xinjiang and the Silk Road in southern Xinjiang. The Silk Road could be divided into South Road and North Road, which were integrated at one place, climbing over the Pamir Mountains and continuing forward. The junction of the two Silk Roads was the city wall developed earliest in the West Regions, whose name was “Shule,” and which is the westernmost city in China today—Kashgar.

This was a place where all travelers, explorers, Buddhists, and merchants had to stop. No matter going out or in, they all suffered this harsh testing ground, but they faced greater tests before, maybe the Pamir, maybe the Takla Makan Desert. Therefore, they purged their souls, picked themselves up, and prepared to risk their lives on the road again.

For many people, this was the last station for their lives; for others, this was the new starting point, filled with generousness, fortifying them for their subsequent departure. Whether as terminal or starting point, here was a place for heroes’ pouring wine and sacrificing. Every inch of air in Kashgar bore the dumb guttural sounds of such men.

The world yearned for the connection from here time and time again, but the plateau here was difficult to be climb over. At the foot of Kunlun Mountain, a tall local man told me: “Here the road is remote and dangerous. It takes seven days from some villages to the town, even by donkey. The greatest wish of a wife was to go to the town, but her husband did not allow. He thought that his wife was so beautiful, how could she come back again after walking such a long way? Several decades later, her husband died, but she was too old to go to the town.”

However, these wives and husbands all saw that some persons always walked through their villages. Did they go to the township? Did they go to the county? Or even a much farther place?

Recently, I and my wife went to Kashgar again. Such plump feeling along the way was incomparable. I just want to repeat the words said years before: if the history you want to study is not the common kind but the “great history,” if the literature you want to engage in is not the common kind but the “great literature,” please go to the West Regions, go to Xinjiang, and go to Kashgar.

III · The centre of the centre

WHEN ZHANG QIAN OPENED the West Regions more than two thousand years ago, he found that Kashgar had a very decent commercial market. Later, Ban Chao, of the “Protectorate of the West Regions” in Han Dynasty, made it the supreme headquarters for maintaining the West Regions, and he even lived there ten years.

When Ban Chao came here, the spiritual culture of the local people remained the primitive natural religion of shaman witchcraft. However, soon after he left there was a significant cultural event: Buddhism from India began to spread to China on a massive scale, and this became its primary corridor.

As for Buddhism’s eastward spread, I have always believed it was a very significant event in the history of human culture. The reason: as it spread to China, it achieved an integral, self-sufficient concentration of spirit. Since a hundred schools of thought contended in the Han Dynasty, it seemed that all their minds had been filled up, and so how could they receive such a completely unknown civilization devoutly outside Mount Guan from thousands of miles away? However, thanks to the mutual dignity of Indian and Chinese civilizations, the psychology of painful precaution was finally overcome step by step. Kashgar must have been the first place to feel such pain, and the first place to overcome it.

After two hundred years, or the fourth century AD, this was already a place of prosperous Buddhist culture, with many historical sites and deeds. For example, Kumarajiva, whose contributions to Chinese Buddhist history were as great as Xuanzang, came here when he was twelve years old and studied Hinayana for two years; also here, he met Wangzi and Canjun from Shache, who mastered Mahayana, and so he turned to Mahayana and passed that on all through his life. Shache now belongs to Kashgar, too. Although the main Buddhism in Kashgar was always Hinayana, and Kumarajiva had to leave, it would always be the place for his spiritual transformation.

Soon after Kumarajiva left, Fa Hien came here for the scriptures and proclaimed the grand Dharma assembly here. Later, with regard to the scriptures retrieved by Xuanzang, he also stayed here for a period to copy some scriptures damaged by water.

During the Karakhanid Empire in the ninth to thirteenth centuries AD, surpassing culturally creative capability was shown in Kashgar, including the long narrative poem “Kutadgu Bilig,” the first work written with pure Uighur script, and the elaborate Turkic Dictionary, which were crucially important Uygur culture classics. Following them were many excellent works. Kashgar shines with sacred glory in the creation of classics.

In fact, Kashgar was also the front line of Islam’s introduction to China in the seventh century AD; Islam did not spread to the north and Xinjiang until setting down its roots in Kashgar for hundreds of years. The cultural relics of Islam there are too numerous to mention, for its main belief up to today was Islam. Over thousands of years, it was nourished by devout rites every day, and the ruins also shared in this life, so all would brim with energy and vitality.

According to the records of Marco Polo, a traveler from Europe, the Nestorians, one of the religious sects of Christianity, named as “Nestorianism” in China, included many adherents here, and its religious services continued in a satisfactory manner, though it had been banned by Rome in the fifth century AD. About such matters, the Italian Marco Polo was very sensitive. Also, Zoroastrianism, long banned by Persia in ancient times, was very popular in the civil society of this region; even the Book of Southern Tang reported that “Zoroastrianism is embraced” in Shule area.

In general, Kashgar was a clearinghouse not only for the commodity trades, but also for spiritual culture over thousands of years. It had a large range that included Central Asia, South Asia, West Asia, and faraway Europe. If the West Regions was the integration center of major civilizations, Kashgar was the center of the center.

The place had existed since ancient times, but only quietly in the hearts of traveling merchants. In the nineteenth century, the world gained new consciousness of that realm, and so the significance of Kashgar again aroused widespread attention. At that time, many of the leading scholars in the world firmly believed that many significant cultural footprints must have been left there, so they traveled thousands of miles and came to Kashgar. As Tachibana Zuicho, an explorer from Japan, said: “Here is the political and commercial center in Central Asia, known by the world long before. Now all people who travel to Central Asia will certainly appreciate Kashgar.”

When looking over archeological works in the world, you find that Kashgar has become a commonly used noun that cannot be avoided in Orientalism research.

In the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, domestic strife and foreign aggression troubled China greatly; added to those deep distresses, China was almost completely partitioned by the Great Powers. However, even at such a time, an archeologist who was sent to innermost Asia but never came to Kashgar was like a graduate whose diploma had never been stamped with the signature and seal of the president.

History is easy to forget, but is difficult to forget it entirely. In those wanderers’ nights, when batches of foreign tipplers rejoiced at the sandhill, the sandhill under their feet sighed desolately and coldly revealed the scrap of historical ruins, which seemed to remind them what this place was.

IV · Two consulates

IN APRIL 1881, the Russian consulate in Kashgar was established. It was a commonplace post originally, but strangely enough, sixty Cossack cavalries were quartered there. These cavalries crossed the large square in the city to the riverbank of the east city for practice, twice in the morning and evening every day, and also performed swordplay, equestrian feats, and marksmanship for the crowds. The consul in the Russian consulate was quite learned, and his name was Petrovskiy. A British scholar describes him as follows:

Petrovskiy, efficient, arrogant, cunning and skillful in ensnarement, used every means including conspiracy, intimidation, threat, inducement, bribe and compulsion with Chinese officials during the twenty-one years of his tenure. His aim was to partition the oasis in the westernmost Xinjiang of China, so that Russia would be able to control the strategic mountain pass to the Indian postern.

—Janet Miskiw, Archeology and Exploration of Stein

Russia’s expectation to control the postern to India was clearly a provocation to England. At that time, besides implementing colonial rule in India, England also controlled Kunlun Mountain, the Hindu Kush, and majority of districts south of Amu Darya. How could it allow the intervention of Russia? Therefore, the later British consulate general in Kashgar occupied twice the floor area of Russia’s, and it was even much grander than its consulate in Urumchi. A British journalist wrote:

During over fifty years of great competition and fighting for Central Asia between the British Empire and Tsarist Russia, Kashgar was always a forefront position for the British Empire. In that competition, the British Empire carried out a long and complicated battle with Tsarist Russia, so as to obtain dominance of the politics and economy in Asia. That British flag waving on the British consulate in Kashgar was the only one between India and the North Pole.

—Peter Holbrook, Reminiscence of a Diplomatist Wife to Kashgar

During the confrontation between Cossack cavalries and the British flag in Kashgar, every day scholars of the past thousands of years also came to this city. Sven Hedin came, and setting off from here, he found the ancient city—Dandan Oilik, thousands of years old—and investigated the migration sites of Tarim River and Lop Nur. Along with the achievements of Sven Hedin, Stein also came and found the remains of the Gandhara—“Hellenistic Buddhism art”—and the Loulan ruins. Such a series of relics showed the incomparable significance of this land from ancient times in many different ways.

This “incomparable significance” can be divided into two kinds. The first kind ends with ancient times while the second kind extends to modern times. Most cultural relics found in the West Regions are of the second kind. Just like rows of huge mathematical formulae left by the ancient sages, they are proving the necessary connection between major spaces, and the practical possibility of opening up such necessary connections. After the archeological discoveries in the West Regions, the historian Wales made a judgment: “I begin to know as of today that the Tarim River is much more important than the Jordan River basin and Rhine Valley.”

It was just such judgment that roused the Cossack cavalries and the British in Kashgar. The consuls from the two countries would receive those archeologists affably, and seek for them to provide more ancient reasons for the modern ambitions of their separate empires. However, the archeologists had little respect for those two consuls. After all, they knew the history very well and understood the severity of their tasks compared with the politics of these diplomats. They went deep into the desert and would drop to their knees if they saw even a little ancient trace, scrabbling it gently and wiping it off carefully with their hands. They knelt there for a long time.

In this way the archaeologists knelt and gave thanks on behalf of all modern people. On this dumb earth, many places are worth kneeling down at, and many places are worth giving thanks for.

V · The white banner

AMONG MANY “ANCESTORS” in Chinese civilization, the place with the most stunning geography is the West Regions, including Kashgar.

Such words may lead to sidelong glances by other “ancestors,” and I am very sorry about that, but in fact I do not give such praise lightly. Consider Mount Tianshan, Kunlun Mountain, and Taklimakan Desert, real-world structures; just a glimpse of any corner is sufficient to make the people hold their breath. However, here they are arranged together, connected together, and echoing together neatly. What a scene.

A series of unsurpassable impasses, a layer of incomparable magnificence, and a flow of unique greatness surrounds you, conquers you, smashes you, but accepts you nonetheless. You are lost and found again, but it is not the same you.

Facing Mount Tianshan and Kunlun Mountain, high mountains backed by other “ancestors” are just like the bonsai. Facing Taklimakan Desert, the lonely smoke in the desert and the river sunsets chanted about by other “ancestors” are mere childishness.

If you come to Kashgar, you should not select the usual crowded tourist attractions, but the Chogori, Muztagata Mountain, Oytag Glacier, Khunjerab Pass, Yakeailike beacon tower, the millennial euphratica poplar forest that spreads everywhere, and the desert at sunset. I and my wife are fascinated with the music of the Twelve Muqam in Shache, which makes us yearn for it in our hearts every time. It is no wonder that it is declared one of the world’s intangible cultural heritages though hidden far away. It makes me think of the Shule music and Qiuci music that caused a sensation in Chang’an during the Sui and Tang Dynasties. Among the grand harmonies of the greatest ancient dynasties in China, it is the music and dance from the West Regions that occupy the leading position.

In fact, besides Kashgar, many other West Region attractions in Xinjiang are worth visiting repeatedly, such as Qiuci (now Kuqa), Khotan (now Hotan), Gaochang, Jiaohe, etc. If you have sufficient energy, you can visit Loulan, Milan, and the Niya ruins.

By the Yarkand River, a local officer had already placed the brush and Xuan paper in the proper arrangement, to let me inscribe some words and prepare to engrave them in the mountainside. I asked him what words to write, and he said, “Top City in Kunlun.”

I said, “Everwhere here is magnificent.”

After I finished it, my view spanned the euphratica forest, glowing like a row of flames, the layers of clouds, and the heavenly road on Kunlun Mountain. That road led to the Ali District in Tibet. Suddenly I saw plumes of white smoke rising into the blue sky from the snowy peak. Could there be someone living there?

“How can white smoke appear among such high clouds?” I asked.

The master said it was not white smoke, but the snowcap being blown by the wind.

So that was it. Yet on second thought, my doubts have been shared by travelers in past times. They were guessing and judging, looking down the road sometimes, and raising their heads again. How can a place without people have white smoke? They probably could not ask it of anyone, and so stayed in doubt, but then looked back again.

In this way, such magical “white smoke” becomes the white banners and flags tantalizing the faraway guests.

I smiled, and thought that the soul of Mr. Toynbee, yearning for the West Regions, now must find its destination among the white banners and settle down delightedly.
